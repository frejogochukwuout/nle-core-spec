### 3.1 nle-engine — `rippleTrimItem` EXISTS (method-level; NOT on the wire; zero tests)

- **EXISTS:** `src/lib/nle/timeline/timeline.ts:2839` `rippleTrimItem(clipId, handle, trimDelta, options?)`, impl `:2853-2963`, docblock `:2818-2837`. Deep-read semantics:

START handle — trim start, **restore the anchor's original `from`** (`:2908`, companions too `:2910-2914`), shift downstream by the DURATION change (`shift = -actualFromDelta`, `:2916`) — the frozen-left-edge / head-removal model (DaVinci-aligned).
  - **Linked companions (`:2864-2889`):** `getLinkedItemsForEdit` gathers the A/V group; clamp via `clampTrimAmount` + `keepTightestDelta` across ALL synced companions (`:2871-2876`); same trim applied to each.

  - **No adjacent clamp — deliberate (`:2834-2835` doc):** "Ripple trim does NOT clamp to adjacent items (it's SUPPOSED to push them out of the way)."
  - **Sync-lock propagation (`:2953-2962`):** `_propagateRemovedIntervalToSyncLockedTracks` / `_propagateInsertedGapToSyncLockedTracks` — a **SIMPLIFIED flat-shift port** ("full port … would split items straddling the interval" — `:2953-2956`).

## 6. VERDICT

Ripple trim is the corpus's best-specified worst-implemented mode: the **wire layer states it completely twice** (spec 15 §4.3.2's `TrimCommand{ripple}` with the exact downstream close/open-gap semantics, mirrored in 06 §5.2A and 16 §3.4's ⌥[ / ⌥] rows with a targeting law), the **keyboard half is genuinely live end-to-end** (nle-ui ⌥[ → follower-shift laws → the app's engine world, one-batch one-history composition, all test-pinned), and the mock's semantic core — trim one edge, downstream shifts by the same delta, upstream frozen, total length changes — is executed faithfully and identically by FIVE independent implementations (engine `rippleTrimItem`, mini R18e/R18f, variants R15-T4 `rippleTrim`+`rippleDeltaBounds`, nle-ui `trimToPlayhead`, app `engineService`). But the mode's DaVinci identity — the ripple TOOL making an edge-drag ripple, with the dimmed-downstream/push-arrow/bracket-cursor grammar — exists **only in shell-variants** (one mock repo), while the canonical tree (OT) adopted the classic flag law's delete half and stopped: `rippleMode` routes delete and a split-seek, never trim; the resize gesture commits ripple-blind `updateElements` patches; the `timeline.trim` verb can't even carry a ripple flag; and the engine's faithful `rippleTrimItem` sits dead — off the wire, at zero test coverage, missing the transition/keyframe preservation clamp, with a simplified sync-lock port. The "ripple is a flag, not a command" law is fully compatible with DaVinci's tool-mode in principle (the classic quote explicitly covers trim through the flag); the corpus just never wired that half. The critical path to closure is G1/G2 (OT wire param + gesture routing, r1), which G4's K3 composition rows and G5's affordance laws then consume — everything else is already written, somewhere, and merely needs one home.
