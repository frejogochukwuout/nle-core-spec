## §1 Findings per NLE

### 1.1 DaVinci Resolve (primary reference — official manual [S1])

**During playback — frame-quantized.** The manual defines the playhead as a frame marker, not a continuous cursor:

> "Ordinarily, the playhead is shown in the Timeline as a single line that **indicates the beginning of the frame that you're viewing** in the Timeline Viewer." — *Show Playhead Shadow*, Edit chapter, p.825 [S1]

Playback itself runs at the timeline frame rate ("Playback frame rate: Usually mirrors the frame rate selected in the 'Video format' setting … given the 'Timeline Frame Rate' setting", Project Settings, p.141 [S1]). Because the viewer displays whole frames and the playhead marks "the beginning of the frame that you're viewing," the playhead position is frame-quantized and advances per displayed frame during playback (a ~fps-cadence publish, not a display-refresh tween). The optional **playhead shadow** (User Preferences → UI Settings, "Show playhead shadow," default 5 frames pre/post; the tip suggests 0/1 for a "Media Composer-style playhead that shows the duration of the current frame", pp.825/109-110 [S1]) is explicitly a *measuring visualization in frames around a frame-boundary line* — further confirmation the playhead is a frame-grid object. No preference anywhere offers an interpolated/smooth playhead; the "smooth"-named options are **timeline scrolling** modes, not playhead cadence (see §Q4 row below).

**During scrubbing/seeking — lands on frames (one explicit prohibition).** The single most on-point sentence in any of the three manuals:

> "**While you cannot move the playhead in subframe increments**, you can resize audio clips in subframe increments by dragging an audio clip's In or Out point in the Timeline, or by dragging an audio edit to perform a roll." — *Resizing Audio Clips in Subframe Increments*, p.1106 [S1]

Corroborated at the project level by the interlaced-workflow setting: "Align Clips to Frame Boundaries: … When checked this means **all editing actions and playhead stepping will occur at full frame boundaries** … When unchecked, all editing actions and playhead stepping will be performed at the **field** boundary instead" (p.141 [S1]) — i.e., the only sub-frame playhead *stepping* Resolve supports on the media pages is field-level (½ frame) for interlaced video, still a discrete grid, never continuous. Timecode entry is frame-granular: "The right-most pair of timecode values (or period) you enter is **always the frame number**" (p.877 [S1]). Scrub **audio** is sample-accurate as feedback — audio scrubbing is on by default ("you'll hear audio when dragging the playhead," Timeline → Audio Scrubbing ⇧S, p.825 [S1]), and in the audio-keyframing context: "When zoomed in, you can **scrub the edit timeline in sub-frame increments with full audio feedback**" (p.1113 [S1]) — the *sound* resolves sub-frame while the playhead *parks* on frames (per the p.1106 prohibition). Fairlight's Loop Jog extends this: "a brief sample preview … while scrubbing the playhead through the Timeline … loops endlessly when you hold the playhead on a frame," with Loop Jog Width in **milliseconds** (p.118 [S1]).

**Timecode display — frames by default; sub-frame/samples are opt-in *display* modes.**

> "Timecode Display can Show Frames, Subframes or Audio Samples — You can view timecode in the Edit page timeline with **Frames, Subframes, and Audio Sample** information instead of just timecode. To change the display, right-click on the Timecode display in the Timeline, and select your option from the dropdown menu." — Edit chapter, p.854 [S1]

**The genuine sub-frame surface area is audio, and it is a preference-scoped variant.** Resolve's sub-frame features, all audio/edit-geometry (never the playhead):
- *Subframe audio edit points:* "DaVinci Resolve lets you optionally make subframe audio adjustments to the In and Out points of audio clips … The **'Align audio edits to frame boundaries' preference** in the Editing panel of the DaVinci Resolve User Preferences lets you choose whether audio clip In and Out points align to whole frame boundaries, just like video clips. When this option is turned on, you cannot make subframe audio edits. **When turned off (the default), you can.**" (p.1106 [S1]). The cleanup command exists: Timeline → **Trim Audio Edits to Frame Boundaries** "conforms every edit on the timeline to frame boundaries on any clips that were edited to subframe precision" (p.1106 [S1]).
- *Subframe audio keyframes/fades:* "You can add and move audio keyframes with **subframe, audio sample accuracy on the Edit page**"; "Audio Fade Handles can also be adjusted in subframe increments"; crossfade edges "can be edited in sub-frame increments" (pp.1112-1113 [S1]). The manual's own figure captions describe audio edits "seen within a **one-frame playhead shadow**" — the playhead frame is the ruler against which sub-frame audio edits are measured.
- *Nudge commands:* "One Subframe Forward (Option-Right Arrow) / One Subframe Reverse (Option-Left Arrow)" for slipping audio sync (p.916 [S1]); the "Audio subframe nudge" preference "Determines the number of **milliseconds or subframes** … nudged when you use the Subframe Left/Right controls" (User Preferences → Editing, ~p.113 [S1]); Fairlight has the same "change the nudge amount by either subframes or by milliseconds" preference (p.3878 [S1]).
- *Fairlight sample editing:* "You can zoom quite far into audio clips on the Fairlight page timeline, **until you see the individual samples** … You can non-destructively edit these control points to eliminate clicks and pops" (p.3898 [S1]) — sample-*level waveform restoration*, not playhead positioning. Fairlight's playhead itself: "By default the Playhead moves along the Timeline as it plays. However, Fairlight offers the option of a **Fixed Playhead** where the Timeline moves while the Playhead remains centered" (p.3827 [S1]) — a scrolling mode.
- *The one true sub-frame playhead in Resolve lives on the Fusion (compositing) page*, a different tool context: "The Current Time field … displays the frame number for the playhead position … numbers can be entered in **sub-frame increments**. You can set a range to be –145.6 to 451.75 or **set the playhead to 115.22**. This can be very helpful when **animating** parameters because you can set keyframes where they actually need to occur, rather than on a frame boundary" (pp.1296-1297 [S1]); plus the "Frame Increment Options … move the playhead in **sub-frame or multi-frame increments** … useful when rotoscoping or inspecting interlaced frames one field at a time (0.5 of a frame)" (p.1296 [S1]).

### 1.2 Adobe Premiere Pro

**Default — the sequence is a frame grid; video adjustments are whole-frame.** "While working with video, you work at a set frame rate, like 30 frames per second … **You're also locked to a certain frame rate when working in an Adobe Premiere Pro timeline.** However, audio is different because it runs at samples instead of frames." (ProVideo Coalition, "Tool Tip Tuesday: Show Audio Time Units," 2024 — a tip series produced "courtesy of Adobe" [S4]). Larry Jordan: "**While the minimum video adjustment is always and only a frame**, audio can be moved in millisecond increments when in this mode." [S5]. The Program monitor / timeline current-time readout is frame-based timecode (project default Video Display Format = Timecode; Audio Display Format default = Audio Samples) [S10].

**The sub-frame variant is a first-class user toggle: "Show Audio Time Units."** Official Adobe documentation: "To display timecode in audio units, **Audio Samples or Milliseconds**, select the panel menu in the Timeline panel, and then select **Show Audio Time Units**" (helpx.adobe.com, "Change timecode display format in Premiere Pro," updated Jan 2026 [S2]); identically in the (retired) official reference manual: "display timecode in audio units (Audio Samples or Milliseconds), click the panel menu button in the panel desired, and select Show Audio Time Units" (Adobe Premiere Pro CC Help reference PDF [S3]). In ATU mode the timeline's timecode display **switches units to audio samples**, enabling sample-level audio trimming/positioning — "cutting between words or fading out on a syllable become much easier … Premiere is removing the **audio at the sample level and video at the frame level**" [S4]. Practitioners use it to sync multitrack audio "down to the nearest sample" (Adobe Community, 2023 [S7]) and to nudge audio sub-millisecond [S6][S8]. Video remains frame-locked regardless — the two-unit model is explicit in every source.

**Playback/scroll — the "smooth" option is view scrolling, not playhead cadence.** Preferences → General → **Timeline Playback Auto-Scroll: No Scroll / Page Scroll / Smooth Scroll** (Adobe Community [S9]; Tella explainer: "'No Scroll' means the timeline will not move during playback. 'Page Scroll'…" [S11]). No setting offers an interpolated playhead; the CTI's position is the current frame's position during playback.

### 1.3 Final Cut Pro (tiebreaker — official Apple User Guide [S12])

**Playhead is frame-based.** "Move the playhead to a frame in your project: Move the playhead by clicking or dragging in the ruler at the top of the timeline, or by clicking the timeline background" (Navigate your project [S13]). Frame-by-frame navigation is the offered granularity: "Move backward/forward in one-frame increments: … Left/Right Arrow" [S13]. Timecode entry is frame pairs: "if you type '+1612' … the playhead moves ahead 16 seconds and 12 frames"; entering digits yields HH:MM:SS:FF values [S14].

**Sub-frame exists, audio-scoped, as navigation — with an explicit video prohibition.**

> "**Navigate subframe by subframe** — You can navigate clips by subframes. **A subframe has 1/80 the duration of a video frame** and is a more precise unit of reference when viewing or editing audio waveforms that are zoomed in to the sample level. **Note: You can't make edits to video at the subframe level.** … Move backward/forward in one-subframe increments: Press Option-Left/Right Arrow." — [S13]

**TC display has a sub-frame mode.** "You can change the timecode display to show either **timecode, timecode and subframes, just frames, or just seconds**." (Navigate using timecode [S14]; see also Editing settings [S15]). Audio scrubbing contexts (skimming) exist as their own mode: audio skimming ⇧S, clip skimming ⌥⌘S [S16].

## §2 The convention verdict

1. **Frame-snapped playhead IS the industry convention — unanimous.** All three NLEs define the playhead/CTI as the marker of the frame currently being viewed: Resolve says it outright ("indicates the beginning of the frame that you're viewing" [S1]); Premiere locks video to the frame grid ("the minimum video adjustment is always and only a frame" [S5]); FCP's ruler playhead moves "to a frame" [S13]. During playback the playhead therefore advances **per frame at ~fps cadence** (the displayed picture changes per frame; the playhead marks it), and scrubbing parks on frame boundaries. There is **no mainstream NLE with a smooth/interpolated playhead for video** — the settings named "Smooth" (Premiere's Timeline Playback Auto-Scroll [S9]; Resolve/Fairlight's Fixed/Page/No Scrolling [S1 pp.3760, 3827]) move the **timeline view** past the playhead; none of them tween the playhead itself. Our spec 18 §461 ("playhead never animates — position is per-frame state, not a tween") and the engine's S1-F7 mirror-cadence law (notify only when the published frame-snapped playhead changed) are exactly this convention.

2. **The true flavor-split is sub-frame *audio*, not a continuous playhead.** Every sub-frame capability found is (a) audio-domain, and (b) scoped by a toggle or a display mode:
   - Resolve: subframe audio edit points/keyframes/fades (default-allowed; **pref-toggled** by "Align audio edits to frame boundaries"; plus a conform-to-frames command); sample-level waveform editing in Fairlight; subframe/ms nudge prefs [S1].
   - Premiere: **Show Audio Time Units** — a whole-timeline units toggle (Timecode ⇄ Audio Samples/Milliseconds) for sample-level audio editing; video stays frame-level [S2][S3][S4][S5].
   - FCP: subframe *navigation* for audio (1/80 frame) + a "timecode and subframes" display mode; "You can't make edits to video at the subframe level" [S13][S14].
   In none of them does the **playhead publish** become unquantized on the video/media pages. The only genuinely sub-frame playhead anywhere is Resolve's **Fusion page** — a compositing/animation context (set the playhead to 115.22 so keyframes land "rather than on a frame boundary" [S1 pp.1296-1297]) — a different tool, not the NLE timeline.
3. **The TC display layer is where NLEs surface sub-frame** (Resolve: Frames/Subframes/Audio Samples display options [S1 p.854]; FCP: timecode-and-subframes mode [S14]; Premiere: Audio Samples/Milliseconds via ATU [S2]) — i.e., the industry pattern is "frame-snapped playhead + a display-units variant," never "unquantized playhead."
4. **Scrub audio ≠ scrub position.** Sample-accurate audio *feedback* during scrub (Resolve's audio scrubbing/Loop Jog in ms [S1 pp.825, 118]; FCP's audio skimming [S16]; Premiere's audio scrub [S4]) coexists with a frame-parked playhead in all three. The unquantized D5-style model conflates the two.

**Verdict: keep frame-snapped as primary. The only variant worth registering is an audio-units one (Premiere-ATU-style display/sample-precision mode), not an unquantized playhead display path.**

## §3 Recommendation for our corpus

1. **Default (register as the convention-conformant primary): KEEP the frame-snapped ~fps playhead publish.** It matches Resolve/Premiere/FCP's actual behavior and our existing law set (18 §461 "playhead never animates"; 03's S1-F7 mirror-cadence — notify only when the frame-snapped position changed; 05 §11's frame playhead). No code change required; the gated row can be resolved "keep" with this research as the evidence.
2. **Reject the unquantized display path (the mini's D5 model) as a global behavior.** No mainstream NLE publishes an unquantized playhead on the video timeline; adopting it would be a divergence from every reference, and it breaks the "playhead marks the frame you're viewing" identity (viewer frame ↔ playhead position) that all three NLEs maintain.
3. **If a variant is ever wanted, register it as an AUDIO-UNITS variant in the display layer, priority-ordered:**
   - **V1 (cheap, convention-matched): a TC-display units option** — timecode ⇄ frames ⇄ subframes/audio-samples in the TC readout (Resolve's right-click TC display [S1 p.854]; FCP's display menu [S14]). Pure display mapping over the same frame-snapped playhead.
   - **V2 (audio-precision editing, follow Premiere/Resolve): an "audio units" editing mode** — sample/subframe precision for audio clip edges + audio keyframes only (Premiere's Show Audio Time Units [S2][S3]; Resolve's subframe audio edits + "Align audio edits to frame boundaries" pref as the toggle shape, and a "trim audio edits to frame boundaries" cleanup op as its safety valve [S1 p.1106]). The playhead publish stays frame-snapped; sub-frame is audio-geometry state, exactly as Resolve models it.
   - **V3 (do NOT build absent a Fusion-like context): a sub-frame playhead** — only a compositing/animation page would justify it (Resolve's Fusion precedent [S1 pp.1296-1297]). Out of scope for the NLE timeline.
4. **Scrub-audio parity note:** our audio scrub (the engine's scrub cache/Wave 3F paths) is the sample-accurate-feedback half of the convention; it needs no playhead change — Resolve's "scrub … in sub-frame increments with full audio feedback" [S1 p.1113] happens while the playhead itself still parks on frames.
5. **Adjacent settings not to conflate (register as non-goals for this row):** playback auto-scroll modes (Premiere's No/Page/Smooth Scroll [S9]; Fairlight's Fixed/Page/No [S1]) are *view*-scrolling preferences; Resolve's playhead shadow (5-frame default, MC-style 0/1 tip [S1 p.825]) is a *measuring visualization*. Either could be a future view-state row, but neither touches cadence.

**Bottom line:** frame-snapped publish = primary (industry standard, zero-change). Unquantized display path = rejected. Audio-units variant = the only toggle-worthy flavor, V1 display-mode first, V2 audio-edit-precision second, V3 sub-frame playhead deferred indefinitely.
