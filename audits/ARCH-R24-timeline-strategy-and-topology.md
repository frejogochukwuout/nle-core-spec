## Ruling D28 — the core-module evolution: EVOLVE IN PLACE (the seal-snapshot option registered for run-phase productization)

**Decision:**

1. **OT, nle-engine, and web-daw-core evolve IN PLACE through crawl and walk.** The fork-to-new-clean-base option is REJECTED for now, on four grounds: (a) **the consumer-pin ecosystem** — the engine's OT submodule, the app's OT lock-copy, and the app's engine/WDC vendors all pin exact shas; a fork invalidates every consumer pin simultaneously and forces a coordinated re-pin across three repos for zero functional gain; (b) **the process docs are the coordination surface** — the plan's ORIENTATION cells point at each repo's `.agents/` docs; the queues, DECISIONS ledgers, and SKILL distillations are where the six-stream protocol actually operates (GIT IS THE DISK: the audit trail is the meta-surface); (c) **the seams just finalized IN PLACE** — W11's coverage gate, the engine's fleet-coherence re-pin, and the app's byte-exact lock-copy are the proof that the in-place discipline delivers the seam-finalization the user observed; forking now would reset exactly the evidence that makes forking safe later; (d) the cloudcut lesson generalized: structural churn during seam-proving is the failure pattern; the current fleet froze the churn instead (the drag-law freeze, the D25 freeze, now the census).
2. **The seal-snapshot option is REGISTERED, not executed:** at run-phase productization (the r1-entry gate, or the first packaging need — whichever comes first), the option exists to **tag the sealed shas and cut clean public packages from them** (e.g. an `nle-timeline` package from OT's tree; an `nle-shell` from nle-ui) via export-ignore/docs-reorganization at the package root. That is a future user-visible decision point with its own ARCH round; the pin ecosystem migrates through the normal pin-bump protocol at that time. Nothing before that gate.
3. **Standing:** the per-repo process artifacts (`.agents/`, worklogs, milestone ledgers, gaps/ registers) are load-bearing until the productization gate; repo hygiene debt (e.g. the engine's meta-docs lagging its last 3 commits; nle-ui's DECISIONS gap) is filed as queue items, not as topology changes.

**Rationale:** the fork-vs-inplace question is really "when does the product need a clean base" — and the answer is "at productization, not during seam-proving". The in-repo artifacts cost nothing at runtime (they don't ship); they cost only repo-weight, which is the disk's problem, not the product's. The one real cost of in-place — history/process noise in the working tree — is exactly what the seal-snapshot option addresses later, without sacrificing the coordination surface now.

**Consequences:** the plan's protocol section gains the productization-gate row; spec 00's Decision 15 (evolve-in-place) is EXTENDED (not superseded — R15's decision now carries the seal-snapshot option + the gate); no battery check beyond the existing pin classes (the in-place law is already battery-coherent).
