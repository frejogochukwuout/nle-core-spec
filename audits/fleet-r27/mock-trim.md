### 1.1 ROLL (`:436-492`)

- **Semantics** (`:449-452`, verbatim): "A roll trim works on both the left and right sides of an edit at the same time. While one side is shortened, the other side is extended by the same number of frames so the overall length of your timeline remains the same."
- **Diagram grammar**: BOTH clips of the junction are active — Bird.mov carries `green-edge right` (`:469`), Sunset.mov carries `green-edge left` (`:474`); **neither clip is `.dim`** — two equal participants, the affordance sits on the JUNCTION, not on a clip. Roll cursor = two brackets + arrowheads pointing OUTWARD both ways (`:478-483`). Free two-way arrow UNDER the clip band (`:486-490`, top:294) — the edit point can move either way.
- **Edge cases named**: none (no bounds, no modifiers, no neighbors-beyond-the-pair).

### 1.2 RIPPLE (`:495-551`)

- **Semantics** (`:509-512`, verbatim): "Rippling will extend or shorten the beginning or end of a clip. When you ripple an edit point, everything to the right of the edit is pushed down the timeline or pulled in to accommodate the clip's new duration."
- **Diagram grammar**: the trimmed clip (Bird.mov) carries the ONE `green-edge right` (`:529`); the downstream neighbor (Sunset.mov) is `.dim` **and carries inline geometry** `left: 430px; width: 157px` (`:531`; base sunset = 432/268 at `:196-202`) — the neighbor is drawn BOTH slightly shifted AND substantially narrowed, a didactic composite of "pushed down the timeline or pulled in" (see F-7). Ripple cursor = clip-rect + bracket + arrow (`:537-543`). Push-right arrow below (`:546-549`, top:297).
- **Edge cases named**: "beginning or end" — both edges are ripple-able (the in/out variants); nothing else.

### 1.3 SLIP (`:554-621`)

- **Semantics** (`:567-570`, verbatim): "Slipping changes the portion of a clip that you see in the timeline by moving its &quot;in&quot; and &quot;out&quot; points. The white outline shows the full source clip duration, so you can fine tune it without affecting the surrounding edit."
- **Diagram grammar**: the slipped clip (Sunset.mov) is `.red-border` with `z-index:10` (`:596`, left:432 width:158) and carries BOTH `.glow-l` and `.glow-r` (`:599-600` — the dual-edge slip law). The `.white-box` (`:590`, left:278 **width:440** ≈ 2.8× the 158px visible slot) = the full source duration drawn around the FROZEN slot. The `.preview-frame` (`:592-594`, 148×123 inset +4/+4 inside the outline) = the bright in-point preview (note: it paints the BIRD scene's art — an authoring artifact, ruled by 05 §8A's in-point-preview-frame row). The neighbor (Bird.mov) is `.dim` (`:581`). Slip cursor = brackets + two arrows INSIDE, diverging (`:604-609`, top:129 — HIGH on the clip). In-box arrows at both ends of the outline (`:612-619`) — one pointing left at the outline's head, one right at its tail.
- **Edge cases named**: "without affecting the surrounding edit" (the frozen-slot invariant); "fine tune" within the source-duration envelope.

### 1.4 SLIDE (`:624-689`)

- **Semantics** (`:637-640`, verbatim): "Sliding changes a clip&rsquo;s position on the timeline without changing its length. The clips on the left and right get shorter or longer as you slide the clip in the middle. You can think of a slide like a roll between 3 clips."
- **Diagram grammar**: the slid clip (Sunset.mov) is `.red-border` z-10 (`:662`, left:430 width:158); BOTH neighbors are `.dim` (Bird `:651`, Surf `:658`). TWO `.white-box`es = the **neighbor shrink boxes** (`:668-669`: left box 147×76 at left:283, right box 141×76 at left:588 — both `top:191`, i.e. anchored on the clips' LOWER/title band), with direction arrows inside each pointing OUTWARD (`:680-687`). Slide cursor = arrows OUTSIDE the brackets and "sits low, over the title bar" (the file's own comment `:671`; cursor at `:672-677`, top:185 — LOW on the clip). The slip-HIGH / slide-LOW glyph posture is deliberate.
- **Edge cases named**: the 3-clip frame (left neighbor + slid clip + right neighbor); "without changing its length."
